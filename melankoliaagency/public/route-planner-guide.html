Section A — What the Route Planner is

An all-in-one workspace that bridges geographic viability and financial success — no more juggling maps, spreadsheets, email and a CRM.

At its core the planner is map-first. As you define the artist’s constraints — travel preferences, party size, gear weight — it visualizes realistic transit paths across actual roadways, warning you of impossible drives or budget-draining detours before you ever send a pitch.

Combined with a granular outreach board, you can pitch multiple venues in parallel for any date, keep backup holds active, and generate agency-branded, personalized inquiry emails in one click. Whether it’s an intimate club run or a multi-bus campaign, this is your mission control.

Section B — Step-by-step: booking a Sacred Skin US West Coast run

The scenario. Sacred Skin is a darkwave/post-punk band on the Melankolia roster, travelling as a 4-piece in a cargo van with ~300 lbs of backline, seeking a door-split-versus-guarantee deal. We’ll route a 5-show August 2026 run from Los Angeles to Seattle, built around a Friday-night anchor festival in San Francisco.
1

Open the Tour Library & start a project

Log in to the Melankolia Ops Platform and open Route Planner from the sidebar. The left Tour Library panel is your archive of draft, confirmed and historic runs. Click + New Tour at the top to open a fresh, blank configuration sheet.

2

Configure the Route Builder form

In the Route Builder on the left rail, enter Sacred Skin’s parameters: artist Sacred Skin, region US West Coast, dates Aug 2026, show count, start Los Angeles, end Seattle, travel Van (ground), party size 4, gear ~300 lbs, and a door-split-versus-guarantee deal type.

3

Establish your anchor shows

Lock your non-negotiable dates first so the engine routes around them. In Anchor Shows, add the Friday Aug 14, 2026 slot at San Francisco Gothic Fest (Grand Ballroom) with its guarantee, marked Confirmed.

4

Generate the AI route leg list

Click Generate Tour. The engine processes live highway networks, venue histories and drive guidelines to output a day-by-day itinerary: Los Angeles → San Francisco (anchor) → Sacramento → Portland → Seattle. Each stop lands in the Booking Pipeline board (Prospects → Holds → Negotiating → Confirmed) with its target rate and projected net.

5

Analyze real driving paths & transit times

The map now draws real, highway-specific driving paths between stops. Each leg shows the true metrics: LA→SF (~380 mi, ~6 h), SF→Sacramento (~90 mi, ~1.5 h), Sacramento→Portland (~580 mi, ~9 h), Portland→Seattle (~175 mi, ~3 h). Flights render as dashed lines.

6

Review with AI Oversight & the Fix chat

The Sacramento→Portland leg is a grueling 9-hour drive. Click AI Oversight to scan for risks — it flags the long leg. Use the inline Ask AI / Propose Fix box: type “add a travel/off-day in Ashland, OR between Sacramento and Portland”, review the proposed change summary, and click Apply.

7

Expand stop details & the Outreach Board

Click the Portland stop in the leg list to open the Stop Detail pane and its nested Outreach Board. Compatible darkwave venues are pre-pulled: Holocene (325), Star Theater (450), Mississippi Studios (300).

8

Manage parallel outreach & keep backups

Pitch all three Portland venues to move them from To contact to Contacted. When Holocene offers a strong versus deal, set it Confirmed and flip the others to Backup — their holds stay on file, ready if the primary falls through.

9

Monitor the tour-wide Outreach Board

Open the Outreach Board tab for a pipeline overview. The KPI strip shows confirmed / in-conversation / follow-ups due. Hit Draft all follow-ups to batch-generate warm nudges for anyone contacted 7+ days ago, and watch the hold-deadline alerts.

10

Save & persist your tour

Click Save Tour. Your map, driving lines and outreach boards persist to the Melankolia database, and draft show records are auto-created in your advancing database — bridging booking into production.

Section D — Glossary of touring & booking terms

A shared vocabulary for deals, show-day production, holds, and travel/legal admin.

Deal & Financial Terms

Guarantee
A fixed, pre-negotiated fee that the promoter or venue guarantees to pay the artist for their performance, regardless of ticket sales or show attendance.
Door Deal
A financial arrangement where the artist does not receive a guaranteed fee, but is instead paid a set percentage of all ticket revenues collected at the entrance.
Door Split
A type of door deal where the gross or net box office receipts are split between the venue and the artist at a specified percentage, such as a 70/30 split in favor of the artist.
Versus (Vs) Deal
A deal structure where the artist receives either their flat guarantee or a agreed percentage of the ticket revenue, whichever amount is higher at the end of the night.
Backend
The financial revenues or percentages earned by an artist after the show's production expenses have been fully recouped and the initial guarantee has been covered.
Plus Deal
A financial structure where the artist receives a flat guarantee *plus* a percentage of ticket sales after the promoter's expenses and a pre-negotiated promoter profit are deducted.
Breakeven
The exact point at which ticket revenues match the total show production expenses (the "nut") and artist guarantees, resulting in zero net loss or profit.
Settlement
The meeting at the end of a show night between the tour manager and the promoter or venue representative to calculate final ticket sales, deduct expenses, and pay the artist.
Guarantee vs Percentage
A deal type identical to a Versus Deal, where the artist is paid either their flat guarantee or a set percentage of sales, depending on which is larger.
Deposit
An upfront payment (typically 10% to 50% of the guarantee) paid by the promoter to the booking agency before the show date to secure the booking.
Net vs Gross
Gross refers to the total revenue earned from ticket sales before any deductions; Net refers to the remaining revenue after taxes, credit card fees, and venue expenses are deducted.
Capacity
The maximum number of patrons legally allowed inside a venue, which determines the potential gross box office revenue.
Sold Ticket Count / Walk-Up
Sold ticket count is the total number of tickets purchased in advance or at the door; walk-up refers specifically to tickets purchased at the box office on the night of the show.
Comp / Comps
Short for complimentary tickets; these are free tickets allocated to the artist, venue, press, or promotional partners that do not generate box office revenue.
Nut (The Nut)
The total operating expenses of putting on a show (including venue staff, sound/lights, security, and marketing) that must be recouped before net splits are paid.
Merch Cut / Merch Split
The percentage of merchandise revenue that a venue retains (often 10-20% for soft goods like shirts, and 10% for hard goods like CDs/vinyl) for allowing the artist to sell on-site.
Ticket Scaling
The practice of pricing tickets differently based on seat location, demand, or purchasing tiers (e.g., General Admission vs. VIP).
GBOR (Gross Box Office Receipts)
The total money collected from all ticket sales for a show before any expenses, taxes, or facility fees are deducted.
NBOR (Net Box Office Receipts)
The ticket revenues remaining after mandatory deductions like sales tax, facility fees, and credit card processing fees have been subtracted from the GBOR.
Buyout
A cash payment given to the tour crew in lieu of providing catering, meals, or hotel rooms directly.
Per Diem
A daily cash allowance given to touring band and crew members to cover individual food and personal expenses while on the road. ---

Show-Day & Production Terms

Load-In
The scheduled time when the tour crew arrives at the venue to unload instruments, gear, and production equipment from their vehicles.
Load-Out
The process of packing up and reloading the band's gear and production equipment back into the vehicles after the performance concludes.
Soundcheck
The pre-show rehearsal period where the band and sound engineers test instruments and vocal microphones to balance the audio levels in the room and monitors.
Backline
The heavy musical equipment (such as drum kits, guitar amplifiers, and keyboard stands) provided by the venue or rented locally so the band doesn't have to transport them.
Rider (Hospitality vs Technical)
A technical rider specifies the exact audio, lighting, and stage requirements for the performance; a hospitality rider details the food, drinks, and dressing room requests for the artist and crew.
Day Sheet
A daily itinerary distributed to the tour party outlining the schedule, venue address, load-in times, contact info, hotel details, and curfew for that specific day.
Green Room
A private dressing room provided by the venue for the artists to relax, prepare, and eat before and after their performance.
In-House PA
The permanent sound reinforcement system (Public Address system) owned and operated by the venue, including main speakers, subwoofers, and monitors. ---

Hold & Booking Status Terms

Hold
A reservation placed by an agent on a specific date at a venue while they attempt to build or finalize the surrounding tour dates.
Challenge (on a Hold)
A process where a second agent tries to book a date that is already held; the first holding agent is given a strict deadline (usually 24-48 hours) to either confirm the date or release it.
First / Second Hold
A ranking system for date reservations; a first hold has the primary right of refusal, while a second hold will get the slot if the first hold is released or loses a challenge.
Anchor Date
A high-profile, high-paying, or locked-in date (such as a festival appearance) that forms the structural foundation of a tour itinerary around which other shows are routed.
Routing
The geographic sequence of show dates that minimizes travel time, distance, and expenses for a touring artist.
Off Day / Rest Day
A non-performance day in the tour schedule dedicated to travel, equipment maintenance, or resting the band and crew. ---

Travel, Legal & Admin Terms

Advance / Advancing
The process where the tour manager contacts the venue's production manager 1-2 weeks before the show to finalize schedules, technical specs, security, and hospitality details.
Radius Clause
A contractual restriction that prevents an artist from performing within a certain physical distance (e.g., 50 miles) of the venue for a specified time before and after the show.
Promoter
The individual or organization responsible for financial underwriting, local marketing, and overall execution of a live concert event.
Buyer
A professional who licenses or contracts talent for events, venues, or festivals (interchangeable with talent buyer).
Talent Buyer
An executive hired by a venue or promotion company to curate events, negotiate performance contracts, and book musical acts.
On-Sale
The official date and time when tickets are made available for purchase by the general public.
Drive Time / Drive Nut
Drive time is the actual duration spent traveling between cities; drive nut refers to the calculated cost of vehicle wear, gas, toll fees, and driver hours required to complete a leg.
Immigration / Visa (P-2 / O-1 for US)
Legal authorizations required for non-US artists to perform in America; a P-2 is for exchange artists, and an O-1 is for individuals of extraordinary ability.
Carnet
An international customs document (often called a merchandise passport) that allows touring artists to temporarily import and export musical instruments and gear without paying duties or taxes.
VAT / Withholding Tax
Value-Added Tax is a consumption tax added to tickets/goods in foreign territories; withholding tax is a percentage of the artist's performance fee withheld by the local government for tax compliance.
